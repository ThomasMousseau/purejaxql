**Comment:** The performance of $\Phi$TD depends on the frequency sampling strategy, the weighting function, and the choice of distribution family, but the paper provides insufficient sensitivity analysis of these design choices.

**Our Response:** We agree that these design choices matter, and we ran additional sensitivity experiments on Breakout and SpaceInvaders. The results show two points.

First, $\Phi$TD is robust to the number of distributional components $m$ and the number of sampled frequencies $N_\omega$. Across $m \in 10,20,51,100$ and $N_\omega \in 32,64,128,256$, the learning curves are very similar and their confidence bands all overlap; changing these values does not statistically change performance.

Second, the frequency distribution sampling choice matters in exactly the way predicted by the theory. We compare Pareto, Exponential, and Uniform sampling. Pareto and Exponential are the theory-aligned choice, while Exponential the has additional weighting function which acts as to emphasis the probability of sampling frequencies near zero. These two choices perform statistically the same and showing the best performances. On the other hand, uniform sampling is substantially worse, which supports our choice of theory-aligned frequency sampling rather other distributions.
